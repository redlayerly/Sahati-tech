[stage s1]
title = Fundamentals
lead = The core foundations of artificial intelligence: the mathematical language of neural computation, classical machine learning paradigms, deep neural layers, and modern data engineering pipelines.
simple = AI doesn't "think" the way we do. It's software that learns from examples instead of having every rule written by hand. To learn, it needs three things: a mathematical language to describe data, a way to measure its own mistakes and correct them, and good data to learn from. This stage builds all three.
analogy = It's like learning to drive. The math is the physics that governs motion, machine learning is practice in the parking lot while someone corrects you, and data is the hours you actually spend behind the wheel. Memorizing the rules won't help without enough hours driving.
why = Everything later in this atlas, from ChatGPT to AI agents, is built on these same ideas. If you understand the foundations, you understand why a model sometimes gets things wrong, and why bad data produces a bad model.
example = The spam filter in your inbox. Nobody wrote a rule for every scam email. The system learned what spam looks like from millions of emails that had already been labeled.

[pkg 00]
name = Mathematical Foundations
desc = The mathematical bedrock for understanding algorithm mechanics, vector calculus, statistical inference, and gradient optimization.
simple = A computer doesn't see an image or a sentence. It sees numbers arranged in tables (matrices). Linear algebra defines how those numbers are stored and multiplied, calculus tells us which direction to nudge them to reduce error, and probability lets a model say "I'm 90% sure" instead of a flat yes or no.
analogy = Gradient descent is like walking down a mountain in thick fog. You can't see the valley, but you feel the ground under your feet and always step in the steepest downhill direction until you reach the lowest point.
example = When a maps app estimates your arrival time, it's calculating the likelihood of traffic statistically from past data, not following a fixed rule.

[pkg 01]
name = Machine Learning Foundations
desc = Core concepts enabling machines to discover predictive patterns directly from historical datasets without manual rule programming.
simple = In traditional programming, we write the rules, feed in data, and get answers. In machine learning, we feed in data along with the correct answers, and the model works out the rules itself. The biggest danger is that the model memorizes its training examples instead of learning the pattern, and then fails on anything new.

[pkg 02]
name = Deep Learning
desc = Multi-layered neural architectures capable of automatically learning hierarchical representations from high-dimensional data.
simple = A neural network is made of layers of simple digital "neurons". Each layer takes the output of the one before and pulls out a deeper pattern: in an image, the first layer picks up edges, the next shapes, and deeper layers faces. When the network is wrong, the error travels back from the end to the start (backpropagation), and each layer adjusts its weights slightly.
analogy = Like an assembly line: each worker adds a small touch, and when a faulty product comes out, a supervisor walks back down the line telling each worker how much they contributed to the fault so they can adjust.
example = Face unlock on your phone relies on a convolutional neural network (CNN) that turns your face into a numerical fingerprint and compares it with the one on file.

[term lr-scheduler]

[term epoch]

[term cnn]

[term rnn]

[term lstm]

def = A type of recurrent network with "gates" that decide what to keep and what to forget, so it can remember information from the start of a long sequence.

[term gru]

[term autoencoder]

[stage s2]
title = Large Language Models (LLMs)
lead = The core architecture driving the generative revolution: Transformer self-attention, pretraining foundation models, autonomous agent frameworks, and inference acceleration.
simple = Language models like ChatGPT and Claude run on an idea that's surprisingly simple at its core: predict the next word. They split text into small pieces, use a mechanism called "attention" to decide which earlier words matter for the current one, and pick the most likely next piece. When a huge model is trained on an enormous amount of text, abilities that look like understanding emerge from this simple prediction: translation, summarizing, writing code.
analogy = Like a sentence-completion game: "I went to the bakery to buy some..." You guess "bread" because you've read and heard many similar sentences. The model does the same thing, but it has read the equivalent of millions of books.
why = These models are now everywhere: search, education, customer service, programming. Knowing how they work tells you when to trust an answer and when to check it, and why they can "hallucinate" a fact with complete confidence.
example = When your phone keyboard suggests the next word as you type, that's a very small language model working on the same principle.

[pkg 05]
name = Transformer Architecture
desc = The revolutionary self-attention architecture that displaced recurrent neural networks and enabled modern generative AI.
simple = Before the Transformer, models read text one word at a time and forgot the start of long sentences. A Transformer reads the whole sentence at once, and "attention" lets every word look at every other word and decide which ones matter to it. That's how it knows that "he" at the end of a paragraph refers to someone named at the start.
analogy = Like a reader with a highlighter: for each word, they highlight the other words in the text that help explain it, some more strongly than others.
example = In "I put the glass on the table because it was flat", attention is what lets the model link "it" to the table, not the glass.

[term 05.10]

[term 05.11]

[term 05.12]
name = Self-Attention

[term 05.13]
name = Cross-Attention

[term 05.14]

[term 05.15]

[term 05.16]

[term 05.17]

[term 05.18]

[term 05.19]
name = Layer Normalization

[term 05.20]
name = Encoder
def = Transformer sub-structure dedicated to reading input text bidirectionally to produce rich contextual representations.

[term 05.21]

[pkg 06]

[pkg 07]
name = Context Engineering & RAG Systems
desc = Managing a model's limited working memory and connecting it to your sources through search and retrieval, so it answers from current, reliable information, plus memory systems that let it remember across conversations.
simple = A model has a limited working memory called the "context window", and it knows nothing about your company's documents or anything after its training. The fix is RAG: search your sources for passages related to the question, place them in front of the model together with the question, and it answers from them instead of from memory. Managing what goes in and out of that window has become its own discipline: context engineering.
analogy = An open-book exam. The student doesn't need to memorize everything, but has to know how to find the right page quickly. Semantic search is the index, and the model is the student.
example = A telecom company's support bot answers questions about current plan prices from the company's up-to-date documents, not from outdated information it learned during training.

[term 07.0]
name = Chunking Strategies
def = Algorithmic splitting of long documents into semantic chunks optimized for vector similarity search and context budgets.

[term 07.1]
name = Dense Retrieval
def = Semantic search indexing and retrieving document chunks based on high-dimensional vector proximity in embedding space.

[term 07.2]
name = Hybrid Search
def = Fusing traditional keyword lexical search (BM25) with vector similarity embeddings to achieve maximum retrieval recall.

[term 07.3]

def = Secondary neural cross-encoder pass re-scoring retrieved candidates to prioritize the most relevant chunks for the prompt.

[term 07.4]
name = Graph RAG
def = Structuring documents into knowledge graph entity relationships, allowing models to answer complex multi-hop queries.

[term context-window]
name = Context Window
def = The maximum amount of text (in tokens) a model can "see" at once, including instructions, conversation, documents and its own answer.

[term context-management]
name = Context Management
def = Deciding what goes into the context window and what gets dropped as a conversation grows, so the important information stays in view.

[term context-collapse]
name = Context Collapse
def = The drop in answer quality when a model's window fills up with too much or contradictory information and it loses focus on what matters.

[term context-compression]
name = Context Compression
def = Summarizing older parts of a conversation or documents instead of deleting them, to free up window space while keeping the meaning.

[term context-engineering]
name = Context Engineering
def = Designing everything a model is given before it answers: instructions, retrieved documents, memory and tool results, in a deliberate order and amount.

[term rag]
name = RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)
def = Searching external sources for information related to the question and placing it in front of the model to answer from, which reduces hallucination and keeps information current.

[term semantic-search]
name = Semantic Search
def = Searching by meaning rather than matching words, so "how do I get back into my account" finds a document titled "Password recovery".

[term recursive-chunking]
name = Recursive Chunking
def = Splitting a document along its natural structure (sections, then paragraphs, then sentences) until each piece is the right size, without cutting an idea in half.

[term prompt-compression]
name = Prompt Compression
def = Shortening a prompt by removing words that don't affect the result while keeping the meaning, to cut cost and speed up responses.

[term query-expansion]
name = Query Expansion
def = Rewording the user's question or adding synonyms before searching, to raise the chance of finding the right documents.

[term contextual-retrieval]
name = Contextual Retrieval
def = Adding a short description of source and context to each chunk before indexing it, so the chunk keeps its meaning when retrieved on its own.

title = Superintelligence
lead = Superintelligence is the idea of a system that outperforms the smartest humans in almost every field, not just one task. It hasn't been built, and researchers disagree about whether it's possible and when. We present it here as what it is: an open scientific question, not a prophecy.
simple = Every system that exists today, however clever it seems, counts as narrow AI or approaches general ability only in some tasks. Artificial general intelligence (AGI) means a system that learns and performs most intellectual tasks at a human level. Artificial superintelligence (ASI) goes further: a system that surpasses the best human minds combined in science, planning and creativity.
analogy = The difference between a calculator that beats you only at multiplication, a classmate who beats you in every subject, and someone who outperforms every professor at the university combined. The first exists, the second is a research goal, and the third is a hypothesis.
why = If a system more capable than us can be built, the key question becomes how to make sure its goals stay aligned with our interests and under our control. This is called the alignment problem, and it's why major AI labs have entire teams dedicated to safety research.
example = Governments and organizations have started treating this practically. The EU AI Act places extra obligations on the most powerful general-purpose models, and government AI safety institutes test models before release.
ladder.ANI.title = Narrow AI
ladder.ANI.def = Excels at one specific task: translation, chess, fraud detection or face recognition. It can't carry its skill over to another field.
ladder.ANI.status = Exists today
ladder.AGI.title = General AI
ladder.AGI.def = Learns and performs most intellectual tasks at a human level, and transfers what it learns between fields. Some current models come close on certain tasks, and the definition itself is debated.
ladder.AGI.status = Stated research goal
ladder.ASI.title = Superintelligence
ladder.ASI.def = Surpasses the best human minds in nearly every field. There's no consensus that it's possible, or on when if it is.
ladder.ASI.status = Hypothesis
insight.1 = The alignment problem: the more capable a system is, the harder it is to verify that it does what we actually want and not something that only looks like it. This is an active research field today, not a question postponed to the future.
insight.2 = Forecasts vary enormously. Some researchers think AGI could arrive within years; others think it's decades away or not reachable with current methods. Any precise date you read is an opinion, not a fact.
insight.3 = The physical limits are real: training larger models needs huge data centers, enormous amounts of electricity and high-quality data. These constraints shape how fast progress can go.
insight.4 = From a security angle, more capable systems mean stronger attack tools and stronger defense tools at the same time. Cybersecurity is one of the first fields where every jump in capability shows up.
