When you take a photo with your phone, it saves information inside the file that you can't see: the date and time, the phone model, the camera settings and, often, the **GPS coordinates** of where the photo was taken. This information is called EXIF data.

## When does this data reach other people?

The good news is that most major social platforms strip location data when you upload a photo as a regular post. The risk lies elsewhere:

- Sending the photo **as a file or document** on WhatsApp or Telegram.
- Sending it as an email attachment.
- Sharing a link to a cloud folder with the original photos.
- Uploading it to websites or forums that don't clean files.

In these cases the file arrives exactly as it is, along with your home's location if the photo was taken at home.

## How to check

On a computer, right-click the photo and choose **Properties → Details** on Windows, or **Get Info** on a Mac. If you see a latitude and longitude, the photo carries your location.

## Stop it at the source

:::tip Android
Open the **Camera** app's **Settings** and turn off **Location tags**. The name may vary slightly by manufacturer.
:::

:::tip iPhone
Go to **Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services → Camera** and choose **Never**. When sharing from the Photos app, tap **Options** at the top of the share sheet and turn off **Location**.
:::

:::action
1. Turn off location saving in your camera now.
2. Before sending a photo as a file, make sure it doesn't carry a location.
3. Don't send photos of your home or children as documents to people you don't know.
:::
